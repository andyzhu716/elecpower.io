# NSPB Non-Segregated Phase Busbar: Complete Selection and Application Guide

Non-segregated phase busbar (NSPB) is a category of metal-enclosed busbar system where all three phase conductors share a common aluminium alloy enclosure, with each phase isolated by air insulation only. NSPB is the most widely deployed busbar type in medium-voltage power distribution across power plants, industrial facilities, and commercial buildings worldwide.

This guide provides engineers and project managers with a complete technical reference for NSPB — covering how it differs from [IPB (Isolated Phase Busbar)](/blog/ipb-vs-nspb/), typical applications, key specifications, and how to select between NSPB and alternative solutions.

---

## 1. What Is NSPB?

NSPB stands for **Non-Segregated Phase Busbar**. The term "non-segregated" means the three phase conductors are housed within a single continuous enclosure without solid barriers between phases — each phase is separated only by air.

The enclosure is typically extruded or welded aluminium alloy (6063-T5 or similar), providing mechanical protection, earth-fault shielding, and a degree of environmental protection (IP54–IP66 depending on design).

Key characteristics of NSPB:

- All three phases share one enclosure
- Phases are separated by air gaps only
- Enclosure is earthed at both ends
- Lower cost compared to IPB
- Suitable for currents from 1,000A to 6,000A
- Rated voltage typically up to 40.5 kV

### 1.1 NSPB vs IPB: Key Distinction

For a detailed side-by-side comparison, see our dedicated guide: [IPB vs NSPB: What Is the Difference?](/blog/ipb-vs-nspb/)

The fundamental difference lies in enclosure design:

| Feature | NSPB | IPB |
|---------|------|-----|
| Enclosure | Single shared enclosure | Individual enclosure per phase |
| Phase isolation | Air only | Solid barrier (aluminium) |
| Phase-to-phase fault risk | Higher | Lower |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Typical application | Within switchgear, 6.3kV systems | Generator leads, 10kV+ systems |
| Maintenance | Easier | More complex |

## 2. NSPB Standards and Compliance

NSPB systems must comply with the following key standards:

| Standard | Scope | Region |
|----------|-------|--------|
| IEC 62271-200 | AC metal-enclosed switchgear and controlgear | International |
| IEC 61439-6 | Busbar trunking systems (busway) | International |
| GB 3906 | 3~35 kV AC metal-enclosed switchgear | China |
| GB/T 12777 | Busbar for power plants | China |
| IEEE C37.20 | Standard for switchgear | US/International |

## 3. Technical Specifications

### 3.1 Electrical Specifications

| Parameter | Typical Range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Rated voltage | 6.3 kV, 10 kV, 24 kV, 35 kV |
| Rated current | 1,000A – 6,000A |
| Rated frequency | 50 Hz / 60 Hz |
| Short-time withstand current (1s) | 31.5 kA – 63 kA |
| Peak withstand current | 80 kA – 170 kA |
| Dielectric withstand | 42 kV / 75 kV (1.2/50 μs) |
| Partial discharge level | ≤ 10 pC |

### 3.2 Mechanical Specifications

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Enclosure material | Aluminium alloy 6063-T5 |
| Enclosure wall thickness | 3–6 mm (depending on short-circuit forces) |
| Conductor material | Copper (99.9%) or aluminium |
| Surface treatment | Anodised or powder-coated |
| IP rating | IP54 (standard), IP65 (optional) |
| Fire rating | IEC 60332 (flame retardant) |

### 3.3 Thermal Performance

The ampacity of NSPB conductors is determined by:

1. **Conductor cross-section** — Copper conductors typically 80×10mm to 200×10mm
2. **Ambient temperature** — Standard rating at 40°C; derating required above
3. **Installation method** — Horizontal vs vertical, free air vs enclosed
4. **Dielectric cooling** — Air natural (AN) vs force-cooled (AF)

## 4. Applications of NSPB

NSPB is used in the following typical applications:

### 4.1 Power Plant Auxiliary Systems

In thermal and hydro power plants, NSPB is the standard choice for:

- **Unit transformer connections** (6.3 kV/10 kV switched busbar)
- **Station auxiliary switchgear connections**
- **Motor control centre (MCC) busbars**
- **Generator neutral grounding equipment**

For power plant-specific selection guidance, see: [How to Choose the Right Busbar System for a Power Plant](/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-busbar-system-for-a-power-plant/)

For IPB-based generator connections in large thermal units, see: [IPB in Thermal Power Plants: Engineering Applications Guide](/blog/ipb-thermal-power-plant-applications/)

### 4.2 Industrial Facilities

NSPB serves as the main power distribution backbone in:

- Steel plants (blast furnace auxiliaries, rolling mills)
- Cement plants
- Chemical and petrochemical facilities
- Water treatment plants

### 4.3 Commercial Buildings

In commercial high-rise buildings, NSPB (in air-insulated busway form) is used for:

- Vertical riser busbars (2,000A–5,000A)
- Main switchboard to floor distribution boards
- Emergency power distribution

## 5. NSPB Installation and Interface

### 5.1 Interface with IPB

In most large power plants, NSPB and IPB are used together:

- **IPB** connects the generator to the main transformer (high current, high reliability)
- **NSPB** connects the main transformer to the switchgear and auxiliary systems

The interface point typically uses a **transition enclosure** with a phase-matched connection between the IPB enclosure and the NSPB enclosure.

### 5.2 Busbar Trunking Interface

For data center and commercial building applications, NSPB in the form of **busbar trunking** connects to switchgear through standard cable termination boxes.

For data center-specific configurations, see: [Data Center LV Busbar Trunking Selection Guide](/blog/data-center-lv-busbar-trunking-selection-guide/)

### 5.3 Installation Environment

NSPB installation requirements:

- **Minimum clearance** from enclosure to wall: 100 mm (for ventilation)
- **Support spacing**: Typically 1,500–2,000 mm between supports
- **Expansion joints**: Required every 20–30 m for outdoor installations
- **Grounding**: Enclosure earthed at both ends and at all support points

## 6. Common Fault Types and Prevention

NSPB systems are susceptible to specific fault modes:

### 6.1 Phase-to-Phase Faults

Because phases share a common enclosure, a phase-to-phase fault inside an NSPB enclosure is more likely to propagate than in an IPB system. Mitigation:

- Strict dielectric testing before energisation
- Partial discharge monitoring during operation
- Regular thermal imaging inspections

### 6.2 Condensation Issues

NSPB enclosures can suffer from condensation, especially in high-humidity environments or outdoor installations. For prevention methods, see: [How to Prevent Condensation in Enclosed Busbar Systems](/blog/how-to-prevent-condensation-in-enclosed-busbar-systems/)

## 7. Selection Checklist

When specifying NSPB for a project, verify the following:

- [ ] Rated voltage matches system voltage
- [ ] Rated current includes ≥ 15% margin above design load
- [ ] Short-circuit withstand rating ≥ system fault level
- [ ] IP rating appropriate for installation environment
- [ ] Certified to applicable IEC / GB standard
- [ ] Manufacturer has type-test reports from an accredited laboratory
- [ ] Expansion joints specified for outdoor or temperature-variable installations
- [ ] Anti-condensation measures included for humid environments
- [ ] Interface details coordinated with adjacent IPB or switchgear
- [ ] Fire barrier penetrations coordinated with building fire rating
